# One-to-one teaching

Source: Eero Alvar, *How I Use AI to Learn Things* (2026-08-14)
https://youtu.be/kzcI5F4tGiU

Usual learning is many-to-many. Both directions waste effort.

## One outlet teaches many

A book, course, or video made for a crowd cannot be optimal for one mind. Optimal teaching depends entirely on the learner's current understanding: the whole arc from here to the goal, and each explanation on the way.

The path should sit at the **edge of understanding**:

- do not reteach what they already hold
- do not present what they cannot yet take in

Ideally: one teacher, one student.

## One student learns from many outlets

Switching styles, notations, reliability, and interfaces costs mental effort that is not spent on the material.

Deeper cost: **trust**. An unfamiliar outlet makes the brain hedge. It will not fully commit a fact until the source feels proven. Two identical explanations of the hairy ball theorem — one from a trusted teacher, one from a stranger — are not learned the same way.

Ideally: one student, one teacher.

## One interface, many sources

"Only one teacher means only one perspective" mixes up **source** and **interface**. The teacher aggregates many sources and delivers them through one interface. Perspectives are not lost.

With AI, trust is not built over years. It is **engineered**: verification and fact-checking exist so the information is correct *and* so the learner can stop hedging.

## Two principles

1. **Optimized teaching** — fitted to this mind, at the edge.
2. **Optimized allocation of mental resources** — concentrate struggle in the material. The system absorbs logistics: planning, finding sources, verifying facts, choosing order. Do not remove difficulty. Maximize struggle *in the thing being learned*.
